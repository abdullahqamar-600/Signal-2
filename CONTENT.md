The content foundation for every page on the Summit site and every variation. Written to be read as final copy first, reference doc second.

---

## Voice & tone

Confident peer-to-peer. The reader is a franchise owner or operator running a real business; they don't need to be sold and they don't need to be talked down to. Sentences carry weight without performing it. Concrete beats abstract every time. Numbers and places earn their place; adjectives have to fight for theirs.

Three checks before any line ships:

1. Would a Signal owner actually say this to another owner?
2. Does it survive being read out loud?
3. Can a word come out? Take it out.

**Vocabulary the brand earns.** network, operators, owners, room, working, building, run, grow, the next year, the way we work, the home office, the team.

**Vocabulary to avoid.** unlock, journey, leverage, empower, ecosystem, transformative, deep-dive, world-class, exclusive, can't-miss, unforgettable, level up, synergy, immersive, curated, thought leader.

No em dashes. Commas, periods, colons, parentheses, full stops. Numbers as digits in body copy; spelled out only when they open a sentence.

## FAQs page

**Headline.** Common questions.

**Lede.**
The questions people ask before they pack. If yours isn't here, write the home office team.

**Who should attend?**
Franchise owners, executive directors, directors, and the key people on each team. The Summit is built for the people running and growing Signal franchises.

**Can I bring my leadership team?**
Yes. That's the point of the new format. The Summit was rebuilt so owners and their leadership teams could attend together for the first time. There's no cap on team members per franchise.

**What kinds of sessions are offered?**
Executive updates, leadership development, sales and operations training, owner-led case studies, and working sessions on the day-to-day realities of running a Signal franchise.

**What does registration include?**
Access to every session and breakout, food-truck lunches and refreshments throughout the week, and the optional Monday kickoff (Kids Against Hunger and Vala's Pumpkin Patch). Hotel and travel are not included.

**Do I need to attend all three days?**
The schedule is built as one piece. Plan to be there start to finish. Owners consistently say the conversations between sessions are worth as much as the sessions themselves.

**Is the Monday kickoff required?**
No. It's optional and worth your time. If you can be in Omaha by [X:XX PM on October 5], join us.

**Where is Leadership Summit held?**
The Signal Home Office in Omaha, Nebraska. 3880 S. 149th St., Ste. 106.

**What should I wear?**
Business casual for sessions. Bring layers (the rooms run cold). Closed-toe shoes for the Monday volunteer event.

**Can I bring my family?**
Family is welcome in Omaha; sessions are for registered attendees only. If you're bringing partners or kids along for the trip, the home office team is happy to share recommendations.

**Will sessions be recorded?**
Yes. General sessions and most breakouts will be recorded and shared with registered attendees after the event.

## Anti-references

If a draft drifts toward any of these, rewrite the line.

- "Join us for an unforgettable experience"
- "Don't miss this opportunity"
- "Unlock new strategies"
- "Take your business to the next level"
- "Network with industry leaders"
- "World-class speakers / sessions / experience"
- "Deep-dive" / "level up" / "supercharge"
- Restated headings (a section called "What to Expect" with a subhead that says "Three Days Focused on Franchise Growth")
- Generic stock-photo language: vibrant, dynamic, engaging, immersive, curated
- Anything that opens with "In today's fast-paced world..."

## Notes for variation builds

- The hero copy is the single biggest lever per variation. Treat it as a point of view, not a description.
- Optional kickoff (Monday) deserves real real estate. It's the part owners remember and the part the brand wants associated with the week.
- The "two summits, one week" beat is the news of this Summit. Surface it on at least one screen of every variation.
- Day framing (intent, not date) holds for every variation: "Where the business is going" beats "October 6" for the section anchor.
- Numbers and places do work words can't. 3 days. 20 minutes from the airport. October 6 to 8. Use them.
